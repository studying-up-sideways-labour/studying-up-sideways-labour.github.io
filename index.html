Situating Workers within Broader Labor Ecosystems

Tuesday, October 13, 2:30–4:00 PM (local time)
Salon A–C, CSCW 2026, Salt Lake City

About this SIG

CSCW has built a rich body of work on how workers experience, navigate, and resist extractive labor conditions. This SIG asks a companion question: who produces those conditions, and how might we study them?

We'll gather to think through questions like these:

  • What would it take to study platforms, investors, consultants, legal actors, and state agencies with the same empirical and ethnographic rigor we bring to workers?
  • How do we study power when key decisions sit behind NDAs, trade secrets, and closed doors, and when ethics boards are wary of corporate pushback?
  • How do different conceptions of “the worker” lead us to different diagnoses of power and different possibilities for intervention?
  • What does it mean to pursue this work from within universities increasingly funded by the very technology firms we scrutinize?

How the session will run

The session is designed for conversation: a short framing, small-group discussions, and a collective look at where this agenda could go next. Our hope is to connect people working across digital labor, political economy, platform studies, critical data studies, and allied areas, and to seed a community that continues beyond the conference.

2:30–2:45Welcome and framing from the organizers.
2:45–3:15Small-group discussions on methods, conceptions of the worker, and positionality and ethics.
3:15–3:45Groups report back, followed by collective reflection across themes.
3:45–4:00Synthesis and where this agenda could go next.
